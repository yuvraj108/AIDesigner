Problem

What slowed staffing admins down

01

A score nobody trusted

The old dashboard showed “75%” with no reason. Admins re-checked every match by hand, so AI saved no time.

02

Verification was invisible

Missing certificates and register checks sat inside each profile. Admins opened 20 profiles to find 2 problems.

03

Actions were hidden

Approve, Hold and Block lived behind a ⋮ menu on every row, with no way to approve many people at once.

04

Work arrived in the wrong shape

Facilities sent job orders as PDFs that someone retyped, and contract renewals expired without a warning.

Step 01Heuristic audit

Tap to zoom

I audited all 15 screens of the original dashboard against Nielsen’s 10 usability heuristics.

  1. 01

    Visibility of system status (high). Missing documents only showed inside a profile, so problems stayed hidden.

  2. 02

    Recognition over recall (high). The most common action, Approve, was hidden in a ⋮ menu on every row.

  3. 03

    Help users recognise errors (high). An expired contract gave no warning at all.

✦ Key insight

The dashboard showed data but did no work. Three of the six issues were high severity, and all three were about things admins couldn’t see.

2

Step 02Competitor analysis

Tap to zoom

I compared two Swedish staffing agencies and three AI shift platforms from the US.

  1. 01

    Agencies are trusted but manual. Dedicare (since 1996) and LäkarLeasing (20 000+ doctor assignments) rely on recruiters and careful, human vetting.

  2. 02

    Platforms are fast but US-shaped. ShiftMed, CareRev and Nursa use AI for matching or pricing, but are built for US nurses, not Swedish registers like HOSP and IVO.

  3. 03

    Nobody explains the match. AI picks shifts or tunes pay; none of them shows why a person fits.

Sources: Dedicare · LäkarLeasing · ShiftMed · CareRev · Nursa

✦ Opportunity

Fast or trusted — nobody offered both. VitaLink can own the space in between: AI matching that explains itself, plus Swedish healthcare checks, agreements and payouts in one place.

3

Step 03User interviews

Goal: understand how one opening becomes a placed, paid clinician, and where it gets stuck. 5 staffing admins, 3 facility coordinators and 4 doctors and nurses, plus 2 admins shadowed during a shift.

  1. 01

    Admins don’t trust a score they can’t explain. Every “75%” was re-checked by hand.

  2. 02

    Verification is the real queue. Most waiting came from missing documents, not from finding people.

  3. 03

    Facilities speak in PDFs. Job orders arrive as files and email, not forms.

  4. 04

    Clinicians decide on place and hours first. City, travel time and shift type mattered more than pay.

✦ Key insight

The bottleneck isn’t speed — it’s trust. Admins move fast only when they can see why a person fits and what is still unverified.

Step 02Persona

Tap to zoom

I built three personas, one for each side of a placement, and designed first for the admin.

  1. 01

    Nora Lund, 34, staffing admin (primary). Careful and under pressure. She opens 20 profiles to find 2 missing documents and doesn’t trust a bare score. She wants a ranked list with reasons and bulk actions.

  2. 02

    Erik Sand, 46, facility coordinator. Sends a PDF job order and hears nothing for days. He wants a name he can trust, the same day, with no new forms.

  3. 03

    Emma Lind, 41, orthopedic doctor. Signed up and waited a week. She wants fast approval and shifts that fit her hours.

✦ Key insight

All three share one fear: a mistake nobody catches. Nora fears placing the wrong person, Erik fears an unsafe clinician, Emma fears paperwork that never ends.

How might we

Solution

What I proposed

Three ideas, each linked to what admins told me, with a clear way out if the AI gets it wrong.

01

AI Match with reasons

WhyAdmins re-checked every bare “75%” by hand.

How it works

  1. Pick an opening
  2. See talent ranked with a score breakdown
  3. Read “Why Emma”, then Send to facility

Safety net“Rematch” and “Not a fit” sit beside every match. Nothing is sent without a click.

02

AI verification checklist

WhyMissing documents were the real queue, hidden inside profiles.

How it works

  1. AI reads uploaded documents
  2. Lists and profiles show “4 of 6 checks”
  3. People who pass all six can be approved in bulk

Safety netRegister checks (personal number, HOSP and IVO) always need a human “Verify”. Missing items trigger a request to the clinician.

03

PDF job order → AI draft

WhyFacilities send PDFs, and admins retyped them.

How it works

  1. Upload the job order
  2. AI extracts role, city and shifts
  3. The admin reviews the draft and approves

Safety netFields the AI couldn’t find are marked “Not found” instead of guessed.

Design system

Calm teal for actions. Lime means AI.

Tap to zoom
  1. 01

    One colour for AI. Lime marks every AI suggestion, so admins always know when the machine is speaking.

  2. 02

    No black surfaces. Early reviews found black cards heavy for an all-day tool; teal and mint replaced them.

  3. 03

    Never colour alone. Every status chip carries a word — “Ready”, “2 checks left”, “CV missing” — and every text pair meets WCAG AA (4.5:1).

Typeface: Manrope 400 · 600 · 800 · Corners 12 px on buttons, 20–24 px on cards · Touch targets 44 px

What I learned

AI earns trust by explaining itself

01Trust beats speed

A fast match nobody trusts is slower than a slow one. The “Why this match” line did more than any layout change.

02Every AI feature needs a human button

Matching needed Rematch. Verification needed a human Verify. Auto-fill needed a confirm.

03Design what the AI doesn’t know

“Not found” and “2 checks left” made the system feel honest, not weaker.

04Words are design

Renaming Consultants to Talent and Timesheets to Shifts cleared up confusion before any visual change.

The right clinician, on the right shift, with every check in plain sight.
