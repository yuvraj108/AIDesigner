Problem

What stopped seniors from ordering alone

01

Every step adds risk

Each extra screen is one more place to get stuck. When medicine is needed quickly, a long order feels stressful.

02

Independence, not convenience

For seniors who stay at home, ordering medicine on their own means they don’t have to wait for family to help.

03

Trust is the real barrier

Seniors worry about paying for the wrong thing. Clear prices and easy help make them feel safe to order.

04

Built for a different user

Small buttons and a lot of typing are hard with stiff fingers or weak eyesight. Seniors need bigger, simpler steps.

Step 01Market & business research

View full image
  1. 01

    Seniors don’t show up at all. Core users are urban millennials (80%), then Gen X (15%) and Gen Z (5%). Seniors 60+ aren’t a core segment.

  2. 02

    But they’re a large, growing group: 138M → 194M seniors in India from 2021 to 2031 (+41%), and 54% manage 2 or more chronic diseases.

  3. 03

    Apps aren’t built for them: 66% find digital tools too confusing, and only 8% of device users use health apps.

Sources: NSO (MoSPI), Elderly in India 2021 · HelpAge India, Ageing in India 2024 · HelpAge India, Inter-generational Dynamics & Perceptions on Ageing 2025

✦ Key insight

Seniors aren’t missing because they don’t need medicine. They need it most. They’re missing because the app was built for confident, younger users. That’s the growth opportunity.

2

Step 02Competitor analysis

View full image

I compared 6 leading pharmacy apps on the 10 features seniors care about most.

  1. 01

    Voice search: 0 of 6. No app offers full voice search. Practo’s is limited.

  2. 02

    Ultra-fast delivery: 1 of 6. Only Apollo 24|7 delivers in 1–2 hours (Prime). PharmEasy takes 24–48 hours.

  3. 03

    Discounts and prescription upload are everywhere (5 of 6), so competing on price adds nothing.

✦ Opportunity

The two biggest market gaps, voice ordering and fast delivery, are exactly what seniors need most. That’s where PharmEasy can lead.

3

Step 03User interviews

View full image

Goal: understand why seniors struggle to order medicines online, and what would make them confident doing it alone. 5 seniors aged 60+ in Bengaluru, 10–15 minute sessions, 19 open-ended questions across 5 themes.

  1. 01

    Typing is the biggest barrier. Arthritis and small screens make search painful; they wanted to just speak.

  2. 02

    Drug names are hard to spell. English drug names lead to failed searches and wrong items.

  3. 03

    Hidden costs break trust. Unclear fees and charges made users hesitate at checkout.

  4. 04

    Speed matters for urgent needs. Running out of medicine causes panic when delivery is “tomorrow”.

✦ Key insight

The barrier isn’t just usability — it’s trust. Senior citizens don’t just struggle with the app’s design, they don’t trust it enough to try asking for help.

Step 02Persona

View full image

I conducted a persona analysis to understand how different senior citizen user profiles experience friction on PharmEasy, despite differing personalities and motivations.

  1. 01

    Geeta Sharma, 68, is cautious and frugal by nature, fearing typing errors that could lead to incorrect orders and hidden costs that surface only after checkout. She wants full price transparency before confirming any order, needing to see every charge upfront rather than discovering fees midway through the process.

  2. 02

    Ramesh Kumar, 72, is proud and self-sufficient, priding himself on independence, yet he panics during moments of medical urgency when speed matters most. He wants simple, guided navigation that lets him complete orders without needing to ask anyone for help, especially under pressure.

✦ Key insight

Despite different personalities, both personas share the same root fear — making a costly mistake with no one to catch it. This shared anxiety, more than any single feature gap, is what the design ultimately needs to address.

3

Step 03Empathy map

View full image

I mapped what seniors say, think, do and feel while ordering, to find the gap between their words and their actions.

  1. 01

    Says: “I wish I could just tell the app what I need.” Out loud, the app is “too complicated”.

  2. 02

    Thinks: “What if I order the wrong thing?” Worries about health data and whether anyone will help quickly.

  3. 03

    Does: a workaround loop. Searches, leaves halfway, double-checks with a doctor or pharmacist, writes medicines on paper, comes back later.

  4. 04

    Feels: overwhelmed by menus, anxious about delays, uneasy sharing health data, and proud when voice just works.

✦ Key insight

They say “I want to do it myself”, but they lean on doctors, pharmacists and paper to feel safe. The app has to earn trust so they don’t need the backup. The one positive feeling comes from voice working, so that’s the experience to build around.

4

Step 04User journey

Running out of medicine at 7 pm: 40 minutes from trying alone to giving up. Six stages, and every low point maps to one of the three solutions.

View full image
✦ Key insight

The lowest points are search (typing the name three times) and checkout (“tomorrow” when he needs it tonight). By 7:40 pm, the lowest point, the senior gives up and calls their son, which is exactly the moment PharmEasy loses the order.

5

Step 05Information architecture

View full image

I reorganised the app into 4 user-facing areas and 11 sections, supported by 2 backend layers.

  1. 01

    Get started: sign up / login and a home dashboard with quick links, offers and notifications.

  2. 02

    Shop & care: medicines, diagnostics, consultation, and offers & loyalty as the primary navigation.

  3. 03

    Orders & payments: orders with tracking, prescriptions with reminder alerts, and payments with refunds.

  4. 04

    My account: profile & medical history, and support with chat to a pharmacist and care team.

✦ Key insight

Support and prescription reminders now have their own clear places instead of being buried in Account, so help is always easy to find. Behind the scenes, the search engine and AI order prediction power voice search across every screen.

Solution

What I proposed

Three ideas, each linked to what seniors told me, with a clear way out if something goes wrong.

01

AI voice search

WhyTyping hurts with stiff fingers, and medicine names are hard to spell.

How it works

  1. Tap “+ AI” and speak
  2. Check what the app heard
  3. Place the order

Safety netMisheard twice? Pick from a list or talk to support.

02

Essentials in 30 minutes

WhyRunning out of medicine at night is stressful, and normal delivery is too slow.

How it works

  1. Tap “+ AI” → Ultra-fast delivery
  2. See in-stock items and the ₹25 fee
  3. Track it to your door

Safety netNo stock nearby? Show the nearest open chemist.

03

AI assistant + real support

WhySeniors worry about hidden costs and don’t know who to ask.

How it works

  1. Ask a question by voice or text
  2. AI answers or builds the order
  3. A real person joins if needed

Safety netWords like “chest pain” show the 108 helpline first.

What I learned

Simple design helps everyone

01Start with the business, then find the user

Breaking revenue into users, orders and value showed exactly where design could help.

02Every speed feature needs a safety feature

Voice needed a read-back. AI needed a human. Fast delivery needed an honest fallback.

03Trust is a growth metric

Seniors didn’t need more features. They needed to stop being afraid of a mistake.

04Design for real people

Voice made ordering easier for anyone with stiff fingers or weak eyesight.
